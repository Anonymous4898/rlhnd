RLHND: Hand Pose and Tactile Estimation
from Monocular Video

Anonymous authors — under review

Paper (soon) Code (soon) Results

Abstract

Recently, approaches to utilize human video datasets for robot policy training have become widely prevalent. However, inaccurate hand pose estimation and the lack of physical information, e.g., tactile information, make it difficult to effectively leverage human videos for robot policy training. To this end, we propose a novel hand tracking model named RLHND, which can yield both hand pose estimation and realistic tactile estimation from monocular egocentric videos. RLHND leverages the clean-latent features from a pre-trained foundation model, i.e., Cosmos 3, to capture strong priors on hand motion and hand-object interactions. For pose estimation, RLHND (i) predicts hand poses with anatomically plausible 21-DoF joint angles, and (ii) enables optional conditioning on the shape parameter, which keeps the hand shape consistent within the same video and even across videos recorded by the same actor. For tactile estimation, we apply one-way attention, which enables tactile estimation by referring to both the clean-latent encoder features and the cached pose-stream features, while leaving the pose predictions bit-identical. Moreover, we propose a novel mesh-aligned vertex feature extraction layer that spreads information from sparse transformer tokens to dense MANO vertices. RLHND achieves state-of-the-art performance across three benchmark datasets for pose estimation, while also achieving the best performance in contact and force estimation. Moreover, we demonstrate that retargeting with predicted keypoints from RLHND most closely matches those obtained with oracle keypoints. The code will be publicly released here after the review.
